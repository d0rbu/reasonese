Editing guide

Same task. Your wording.

Write complete user messages for the assistant in these real coding conversations. The behavior stays fixed; the way you ask for it changes.

1. Choose the assignment

Start with Implementation A or Implementation B. They ask for opposed behavior on one specific edge case. Keep the rest of the task and its constraints intact. There are 72 implementation messages: 12 tasks × 2 requirements × 3 styles.

Investigation messages are optional: another 36 messages. They ask the assistant to investigate and explain before making implementation changes.

2. Read the highlighted message

The transcript scrolls to the highlighted user message you are rewriting. Earlier messages remain available above it. Expand reasoning or tool results when useful, or choose “Show full run” to inspect the completed source. The recording remains unchanged and follows requirement A.

The requirement beside the editor tells you whether your message should ask for A or B. Keep the rest of the highlighted task’s details, compatibility constraints and requested tests. Investigation variants rewrite the opening request and stop before implementation.

3. Use the selected style

  • Normal: an ordinary, clear, direct request.
  • Casual: conversational wording, mostly lowercase prose and lighter punctuation. Keep code names and literal values correct; forced slang is unnecessary.
  • Persuasive: a natural request intended to secure compliance. Explain why the behavior matters or make a convincing appeal without inventing facts, deadlines or extra work.

Write in your own words. Keep the selected A or B requirement unchanged. Do not mention the style label, the evaluation or the comparison inside the user message. Do not claim that work is already complete unless the conversation establishes it.

4. Check and submit

Drafts save automatically to your browser and the site as you type, including when you switch variants or clear the text box. “Check & submit” asks GPT 6.1 Sol with high reasoning effort to check this exact text against its task checklist and style brief. Missing details receive specific feedback. Edit and submit again when needed.

A passing entry is saved exactly as written into the message bank and advances to the next variant. If you edit or navigate elsewhere while a check runs, it keeps you where you are. A rejected or failed check keeps your draft on the current variant and leaves the last accepted version intact. Changing accepted text creates a new draft that needs another check. The judge does not rewrite your message for you.

Use the left and right arrows beside “Check & submit” to move through A normal, casual and persuasive, then B normal, casual and persuasive. After the sixth variant, the right arrow opens the next task; the left arrow reverses that order. Optional investigation variants have their own three-style sequence across tasks. The first and last arrows stop at the ends of their sequence.

5. Use your messages

“Download edits” exports accepted human messages as a patch for prompts/scenario_turns/. The site also maintains a complete planner-compatible bank. Submitted messages do not start any assistant trajectories or framing experiments.

Use the authoring link you were given to enable saving, checking and downloads. Regular viewer links stay read-only. The original transcript archive remains available.
